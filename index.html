What I study

My work asks how stigma, marketing, and social environment get under the skin — how people come to be blamed for conditions that are largely produced around them, and what happens to their health when they are. Much of it concerns weight stigma and the framing of obesity, alcohol and its promotion, addiction and the language used to describe it, and the design of interventions that change behaviour without adding to the burden people already carry.

Methodologically I am a psychometrician before anything else. A good deal of my time goes into building and validating measures — appearance comparison, social comparison, family-of-origin influences, parent–child communication — and into the experimental and longitudinal designs needed to test whether an effect is real. More recently that has extended to computational and AI-assisted methods for coding and analysing large qualitative and survey datasets.

I founded and direct the Behavioural Sciences Research Laboratory at Monash University, and from January 2027 I take up a Full Professorship and the Vice Deanship at VinUniversity in Hanoi.

Research

Five strands

They overlap more than they look. Stigma is a measurement problem; marketing is a behaviour-change problem; almost everything ends up needing a better scale.

Stigma, blame, and weight

How explanatory models of obesity and addiction shape public attitudes, discrimination, and the treatment people receive. Includes experimental work on how framing a condition — as a disease, a choice, an addiction — moves stigma up or down.

Experiments · population surveys · intervention design

Alcohol, marketing, and the commercial determinants of health

Exposure to alcohol promotion and sponsorship, its relationship to consumption, and what the evidence implies for regulation rather than for individual restraint.

Cohort and cross-sectional analysis · policy evaluation

Addiction and health behaviour change

What actually shifts drinking, eating, and activity — and what the language of addiction does to people who are trying to change.

Trials · longitudinal designs · behavioural theory

Psychometrics and scale development

Building measures that survive translation and replication. Current instruments include the Universal Social Comparison Scale, the Parent–Child Emotional Communication Scale, the Family-of-Origin and Family Intentions Scale, and the UPACS/DACS appearance-comparison scales.

EFA/CFA · measurement invariance · IRT · cross-cultural validation

Computational and AI-assisted social science

Using large language models and computational text analysis to code and analyse qualitative and survey data at a scale that manual coding cannot reach — and testing whether the results hold up against human coders.

LLM-assisted coding · reliability testing · reproducible pipelines

Funding and service

Research supported by competitive grants totalling approximately AUD 5.75 million. I served as Associate Editor of Addiction from 2015 to 2023, and review for journals across public health, psychology, and addiction science.

Supervision

Working with me

I supervise doctoral and postdoctoral researchers in behavioural science, public health, and quantitative psychology, in Melbourne now and in Hanoi from 2027.

What I look for

A defensible design and a real question. I am most useful to students working on stigma, addiction, alcohol and health policy, health behaviour change, or measurement development, and to students who want serious quantitative training — multilevel and structural equation modelling, psychometric validation, experimental design — rather than a thesis assembled from convenience data.

I also supervise PhDs by publication, and I am happy to talk with candidates who already have papers in progress and need a coherent programme built around them.

Before you write

Send a one-page outline: the question, why it matters, the design you have in mind, and the data you can realistically get. Tell me what scholarship or funding you are applying for and when it closes. A CV and one writing sample help. I read everything that arrives with those four things in it.

Current group

Doctoral candidates and postdoctoral researchers in the Behavioural Sciences Research Laboratory work across stigma, health behaviour, sarcopenia prevention, and psychometrics.
